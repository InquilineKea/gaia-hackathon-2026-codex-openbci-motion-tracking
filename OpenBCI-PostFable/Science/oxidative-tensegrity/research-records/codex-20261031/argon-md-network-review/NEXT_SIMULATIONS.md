These are recommendations. The review ran API workers and coordinate/graph readbacks; it did not run additional MD, QM calculations, a network sweep, ADK, Deep Research or AlphaEvolve.

Choose production length from convergence of target observables and effective independent information, not a fixed nanosecond count. Thirty saved post-release frames cannot determine a reliable equilibration time or a complete conformational ensemble. Matched precision sensitivity is a numerical test, not a physical validation. Do not overwrite the existing double trajectory or interrupted diagnostic.

Define the coarse mechanical observable before force-extension or umbrella/PMF work. The target could be a conditional extension/rotation response of a specified complete junction in its collagen scaffold. The C-alpha marker is not that response. Require independent starts, window overlap, reverse/forward consistency, block/replica convergence, and reference Hamiltonian checks. Do not derive a universal network spring constant from an unaligned displacement, covalent bond length or one short CA variance.

## Define three separate regime questions

Connectivity: does the specified collagen graph develop a macroscopic connected/wrapping component? Mechanical rigidity: does the actual energy model support shear/load under the chosen boundaries after its permitted degrees of freedom relax? Relaxation/failure: does a stated dynamical or damage model change its time response or fracture process? An L-BFGS iteration count is a numerical cost/conditioning diagnostic, not a physical relaxation time. A contact-jamming claim needs its own model and evidence.

Specify nodes and edge types: fibre backbone, added covalent connection, background scaffold, and angle/bending constraints. Higher-order angular constraints are not extra ordinary graph edges merely to increase a clustering coefficient. If representing multiway junctions, compare a factor/junction representation with any projected graph so projection triangles are not mistaken for physical clustering.

## A staged network campaign

Stage 0 is a correctness and cost gate: exact component/triangle fixtures, periodic winding tests, the line-cycle normalization check, zero-bond tether energy, and per-system residual/strain-step/Hessian consistency. Independently measure the segment-length/cut distributions rather than assuming they scale linearly with width. The supplied source already records realized z; retain that record. Fix the target-z normalization or explicitly condition every comparison on measured post-cut/post-bridge coordination. Benchmark wall time on small cases before budgeting a large sweep.

Stage 1 is cheap graph-only mapping. Use widths 16, 32, 64 and a reserved larger width when feasible, with independent graph/insertion paths and a coarse occupancy scan. An ordinary independent-bond reference and the project's forced-cut ensemble are distinct baselines. Track largest-component fraction, finite-cluster susceptibility (excluding the giant component under the stated definition), x/y torus-wrapping probability, realized mean degree, full degree distribution, mean local clustering including its low-degree convention, global transitivity, orientation and component statistics. Nested occupancies along one insertion path are correlated; bootstrap whole independent paths.

Stage 2 is mechanics with matched graphs. Separate added edges, segment stretch/bend stiffening, junction angle locking, rupture parameters and prestress. At first use existing widths 16 and 32, then reserve 64 or larger for frozen-design validation. Cross pure stretching, stretching-plus-bending and angular constraints with zero/nonzero elastin/tether backgrounds, using matched base graph seeds and separate stochastic streams for each intervention. Record relaxed tangent response, appropriate Hessian zero/soft modes, nonaffine motion, anisotropy and any properly defined load-bearing backbone. A generic two-core or central-force pebble game is not a rigidity oracle for bending/prestress/tether mechanics. Set numerical tolerances from response convergence and a calibrated numerical floor; no universal z=4, modulus cutoff or extremely small optimizer tolerance is presumed.

A proposed initial screening budget is 12 feasible, space-filling parameter settings per mechanism family at widths 16 and 32, with 8 independent base graphs per setting; treatments can share those graphs as blocks. Six families would be 1,152 graph-treatment states before multiple strain perturbations or solver checks. This is a design count, not a runtime/cost estimate or authorization to execute it. Start with 16 representative states to measure cost and verify convergence, then shrink or expand the screen within a separately specified compute budget. Keep 20% of initial settings and new graph seeds hidden from adaptive selection, and freeze a larger-size validation set. Require at least three properly matched sizes before interpreting asymptotic scaling; otherwise report size-specific operational crossover intervals.

Stage 3 isolates topology within the feasible spatial ensemble. At fixed node count and edge count, vary clustering with degree-preserving spatial moves where they mix; separately vary degree heterogeneity/hub bias while controlling triangles, length/orientation distributions and bending motifs as far as jointly feasible. Log constraints that cannot be jointly matched, acceptance rates and between-chain/mixing diagnostics. Degree <=6 is a hard restriction of the current triangular candidate graph. Growing Barabasi–Albert attachment requires a different graph generator; fixed-node hub-biased bond insertion is a different hypothesis. Do not connect arbitrary nodes and reuse the old collinear-bending arrays. Any generalized spatial graph must rebuild the actual mechanical constraints and be validated against the baseline.

Stage 4 checks putative boundaries across sizes, independent seeds, solver/strain steps and background limits. Compare smooth-response, crossover and physically motivated critical/scaling descriptions on held-out data. A classifier boundary or one susceptibility peak is insufficient. Predefine a minimum scientifically relevant effect and desired boundary precision; a confidence interval containing zero means unresolved evidence unless a properly powered equivalence test establishes a negligible effect. Loading/unloading and rupture-off/on arms distinguish irreversible damage, metastability and numerical path dependence. Physical relaxation tests additionally need a documented friction/dynamical model and time integration; this solver currently supplies static relaxation.

## The advanced swarm controller

Use a versioned hypothesis/evidence board with competing mechanisms: edge-density connectivity, angular/bending stabilization, constitutive stiffening, spatial clustering, hub-biased attachment, and background/prestress effects. Argon high reasoning proposes tests and critiques interpretations; deterministic graph/trajectory/solver checks decide whether artifacts and claims pass. Cheap workers or grounded literature agents can gather candidates, but exact source lines, data hashes and numeric checks remain mandatory.

Fit a calibrated surrogate to the actual simulation observables with replicate uncertainty. Select a next batch by expected reduction in uncertainty about mechanism/boundary per unit measured compute cost. Include new replicas when observation noise dominates; include new parameter points/sizes when model uncertainty dominates. Reserve an exploration fraction (20% is a proposed starting policy, not an established optimum) and keep held-out sizes/seeds/topology families invisible to acquisition. Stop at the compute cap, a preregistered boundary precision/minimum-effect criterion, or persistent failure to distinguish the physical model from a smooth null. Report unresolved regions explicitly.

Optional learned order-parameter discovery can compare a graph/force-field representation, low-dimensional embeddings and sparse symbolic candidates against the explicit physical observables. Score them on unseen seeds, sizes, mechanisms and null ensembles; reject predictors using only node count, simulation labels or acquisition artifacts. A learned phase label does not prove a thermodynamic transition. An AlphaEvolve-style program search becomes useful only after its evaluator, invariants and held-out tests are fixed; searching for the sharpest-looking plot would reward artifacts.
