# Does renormalization work?

There are two distinct questions. The current `run_network3d.py` E2 analysis rescales stiffness to a common initial shear modulus; it is a controlled normalization, not a real-space renormalization-group calculation. A later critical-scaling programme could test a renormalization theory, but current CPU/GPU angular parity establishes neither universality nor a coarse-graining map.

## Matched initial stiffness

For fixed geometry/damage state and displacement-controlled equilibrium, if E(q,F)=sum k_i e_i(q,F) and all k_i are multiplied by one positive scalar s, then E'=sE. Stationary configurations and strain fields are unchanged, forces/stresses/moduli/work scale by s, and a strain-based rupture criterion is unchanged. Thus s=G_target/G_initial is an exact normalization for the represented scalar modulus, provided G_initial is positive, resolved and computed consistently. With the current default zero elastin, the inspected helper scales the nonzero elastic terms (mu, kappa, junction stiffness, embedding stiffness) consistently. This algebra does not independently prove solver convergence or full anisotropic equivalence.

The audit found an actual boundary case: `scale_stiffness` does not scale `mu_el`, although `energy` contains mu*alive+mu_el. At nonzero elastin/background it no longer multiplies the entire energy by s. With mu=1, mu_el=0.1 and s=2.3, the intact axial stiffness ratio is 2.181818 instead of 2.3, a 5.14% shortfall. RENORMALIZATION_CHECK.json records the static source hash and independently calculated counterexample. The correction before using this path for collagen–elastin studies is to scale mu_el as well and test nonzero-background cases independently. This audit does not modify active or historical FCC runs, and the counterexample is not claimed as a new full-solver simulation.

The driver also floors G at 1e-12 before division. That prevents a floating-point division error but does not establish that an almost-zero or negative modulus is valid. The atlas should reject unresolved/negative G and report matching failure; do not amplify solver noise into a huge stiffness factor. Gate dimensionless conditioning and solver residuals, report s, and verify the realized normalized modulus independently after rescaling instead of only multiplying a cached modulus.

Matching G_xy does not match C_xx, C_yy, C_zz, G_xz, G_yz or Poisson couplings. In anisotropic networks, label the specific matched component, report the full tangent tensor, and keep other directional differences as outcomes. Re-fitting each loading direction separately would erase part of the anisotropy being studied. Dynamic relaxation requires a separate decision: scaling elastic stiffness with fixed damping changes time scales. Force-, energy- or rate-based damage laws may also need different scaling; the current strain-based argument cannot be generalized to them.

E1 holds microscopic material parameters fixed and measures total architectural effect. E2 changes specimen stiffness parameters and asks about response after matching one bulk component. Both are useful, but E2 is a computational control rather than literally identical microscopic collagen chemistry. Do not merge E1/E2 into one sample or interpret scaled toughness alone as a chemical/material improvement.

## Actual renormalization and critical regimes

A real-space renormalization/scaling theory exists for idealized fibre-network rigidity transitions. Shivers et al. derive exponent relations and test them on triangular/packing-derived networks: [Scaling theory for mechanical critical behavior in fiber networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01205). That is evidence for a specified model class, not proof for our current anisotropic, crosslink-locked, embedded and damage-bearing FCC networks.

To test it here, first use the reversible elastic branch with damage off. Sweep strain densely around the putative transition, connectivity, bending/stretching ratio and system size with independent graphs. Measure differential modulus, nonaffinity, fluctuations and a suitable length-dependent observable; fit a declared finite-size/scaling ansatz and compare against smooth-crossover alternatives. Fit exponents on training sizes and predict held-out sizes/generator families, showing residuals and uncertainty rather than only a visually tuned collapse. Separate anisotropy components and test embedding/tether and prestress sensitivity: positive tether, bending or locking can round or shift the transition. Only then ask how damage changes that regime. Matching one modulus is not this test.

Primary experimental/model references: [Sharma et al., strain-controlled criticality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07792), [strain-driven criticality and finite-size scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08218), and [Licup et al., stress-controlled collagen mechanics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4534289/). Their experimental conditions and definitions must be matched before treating a model curve as a tissue prediction.
