Oxidative Tensegrity

What happens to tissue when oxidative crosslinks (glycation products such as glucosepane and pentosidine, dityrosine, disulfide scrambling) pile up on top of a healthy, enzymatically crosslinked collagen matrix. One question, followed from single bonds to the heart, with every number below coming from a simulation that actually ran.

The pipeline

Five scales, one chain of evidence

Each scale hands a measured quantity to the next. Crosslink chemistry sets how rigid a junction is; the fibre network turns that into stiffness, strain-stiffening and failure; a tensegrity cell pulls on that network and decides whether YAP enters the nucleus; tissues accumulate crosslinks with age, which sets transport and heart function.

Ångström · crosslink chemistry

Glycated arginine lets go of GAGs

Two atomistic questions run through NVIDIA ALCHEMI with machine-learned potentials. How stiff is each crosslink when you pull on it, and does glycation damage the collagen surface that proteoglycans (decorin's dermatan sulfate) grip? The second gave the clearer answer: arginine is the strong GAG binder, and turning it into MG-H1, the most common protein AGE, halves the contact energy and pushes the sugar out of contact. CML on lysine does not.

Micrometre · fibre network

Branching sets the stiffness; oxidative crosslinks erase the slack

Collagen networks are sub-isostatic: with fewer than four fibres per junction (in 2D) they are floppy until stretched, which is why they stiffen so steeply under strain. Oxidative crosslinks lock junctions, bridge neighbouring fibres and stiffen the fibres themselves. Below, the same 12×12 network is sheared to 40%; segments glow red in tension and the violet dots are junctions locked by oxidative crosslinks.

10–100 nanometre · fibril interface

Crosslinks shut down GAG sliding

Proteoglycans let neighbouring fibrils slide past each other under load, which dissipates energy without damage. In the model, a sliding GAG link slips once it is stretched past 1%; a link anchored at a crosslink-locked junction cannot. Toggle crosslinks in the schematic to see the hysteresis loop collapse.

10 micrometre · the cell

A tensegrity cell needs the matrix to hold its tension

The cell body is a six-strut tensegrity: microtubule struts in compression, actomyosin cables in tension. Its bottom face has no cables, so the matrix, through adhesions, closes the tension loop. On soft matrix the cell cannot hold its prestress and sags; the nucleus feels little force and YAP stays out.

Millimetre → organ · aging tissue

Long-lived collagen ages fastest

Crosslinked collagen resists the enzymes that would replace it, so crosslinks accumulate fastest where turnover is slowest. Tendon and cartilage reach the highest dose by 80; myocardium, renewed within years, plateaus.

What to test next

    Limits

    What this does not show

    Networks are two-dimensional and small (12×12 to 16×16); every parameter is a literature-informed assumption, not a fit. The cell is a single tensegrity unit. Atomistic numbers come from rigid fragments in vacuum with non-reactive potentials, so they rank chemistries rather than predict forces, and the crosslink-stiffness pilot did not fully converge (a converged two-model run is in progress on Brev). The swarm's literature syntheses are leads; several specifics could not be verified.
